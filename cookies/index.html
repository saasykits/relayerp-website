1What these technologies do

Cookies are small values a website stores in your browser and receives with later requests. A session cookie normally lasts for a browser session; a persistent cookie has an expiry time. Browsers can clear, shorten, or otherwise limit either kind.

Local storage keeps values on your device across visits until they are changed, removed, or cleared. Session storage keeps values for a particular browser tab's session, including across ordinary reloads. Browser session-restoration settings can affect how long it survives. Service-worker caches store selected app resources on the device.

Tags and pixels, including Google Analytics and Meta Pixel, run code or make requests to measure interactions. They can transmit information even without a conventional third-party cookie. This policy covers that storage, access, and tracking as well as cookies.

A first-party cookie is set for the site you are visiting. A third-party service can also cause a first-party cookie to be set and receive the information associated with it. A cookie being first-party does not make its purpose essential or its data anonymous.

2Purposes and consent

We distinguish the following purposes:

  • Authentication and essential operation: maintain a signed-in session, apply security protections, and support a service operation you request.
  • Functionality and preferences: record choices such as language, organization navigation, appearance, table columns, and selected form behavior.
  • Optional analytics: use Google Analytics to understand visits and interactions.
  • Optional advertising and measurement: use Meta Pixel to measure events and campaign performance and support advertising-related processing.

Our consent controls block Google Analytics and Meta Pixel until you agree to the relevant optional processing. You can decline optional tracking and continue to use the core ERP. Viewing a page, signing in, or reading this policy is not consent to optional tracking.

Where the law requires consent for a functionality or preference technology, that requirement also applies; describing a technology as functional does not make it automatically exempt. Technologies strictly necessary for a service you request can be used without optional tracking consent where the law permits that exception.

6Changing your choices

Use the Relay ERP consent controls. Review or change optional tracking consent through the controls available on the site. You can withdraw consent for future optional analytics or advertising processing. Withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of earlier processing. If the controls are unavailable or you need help, contact us at contact@relayerp.app.

Manage browser site data. Your browser's privacy settings let you inspect, block, or delete cookies and clear local storage, session storage, and cached site resources. Blocking authentication cookies can prevent sign-in; clearing preferences resets the corresponding choices. Clearing a saved tracking choice may cause the consent controls to ask again.

Apply choices on each browser or device. Browser-based preferences generally apply to that browser and device. Apply your choices again when using a different browser, a private browsing session, or another device.

Distinguish browser signals from consent choices. A browser's “Do Not Track” setting is not the same as recording a choice in our consent controls. Any right under applicable law to use a legally recognized universal opt-out signal, such as Global Privacy Control, is unaffected by this policy. You can also submit an applicable opt-out request through our privacy contact.

Request deletion separately. Removing cookies or withdrawing consent stops or limits future tracking but does not automatically erase data already collected. Contact us or the relevant provider to exercise an available deletion right. Provider advertising preferences can offer additional controls over that provider's wider activities; they do not replace your Relay ERP consent choice.
