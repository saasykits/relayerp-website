How it works

Pass the baton.
Keep the record.

Purchasing hands off to the warehouse, the warehouse to sales, sales to accounts. Every hand-off is a document with a gapless number, and every unit and cent it moves lands in the ledger.

  1. 01 · PurchasingPO-7Purchase orderOrdered once the owner approves it.
  2. 02 · WarehouseGRN-12Receipt500 m of copper wire in, at the order’s cost.
  3. 03 · SalesORD-41Sales orderConfirmed for Harbor Hardware.
  4. 04 · WarehouseDEL-40Delivery320 m out of the lot that expires first.
  5. 05 · AccountsINV-42InvoiceIssued with VAT 15%, numbered on issue.
  6. 06 · AccountsPAY-88PaymentPart paid; the rest stays due on the invoice.
  7. 07 · Cash & bankTXN-311Cash entryPosts to the bank account by itself.

Roles, scopes & sign-offs

You decide who does what, where, and with whose sign-off.

  1. 1Roles from a permission catalogView stock, adjust it, confirm sales, issue invoices. Pick what the job needs; the matching view rights follow.
  2. 2Scopes that match your floorLimit each permission to warehouses and accounts, and to the member’s own records or their whole team’s. Override one person without a new role.
  3. 3Sign-offs that run exactly onceApprovers see the request as it was and as it is now. On approval it runs as the requester, once, or fails with a reason.

The ledger

Nothing is edited.
Everything is explained.

Stock changes only by posting to an append-only ledger. A mistake is corrected with a new entry, so the balance on screen always adds up from the rows above it.

  • STK-3Never below zeroA movement that would take any lot negative fails as a whole, even when two people post at the same moment.
  • INT-3Numbers without gapsOrders, invoices and receipts are numbered when they are committed, in an unbroken sequence per organization.
  • AUD-1Before and afterEvery change records the member, the time, both versions and the approval behind it.
  • SEC-12Walled off by the databaseEach organization is isolated by the database itself, so even a faulty query cannot reach another’s records.
